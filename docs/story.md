# Blobb: story bible

## The frame (never stated outright)

Two narrators are telling Blobb's story. They are an older person and a child, in a hospital at night. The player is not told this; it is revealed only gradually.

**Rules for all dialogue**
- **Equals, not parent and child.** Neither narrator looks after the other. No family words (mum, dad, son, "honey"...) anywhere except the one planned break at the end of ch4 ("...Dad?", the director's decision), and no bedtime or caretaker talk: no "it's late", sleep, tired, "your eyes say...", permission or promises handed down. Nobody asks the other for permission ("Can we...?"), and nobody reassures ("There's no hurry") or grants ("Tomorrow, then"). They decide things together, half each. They build the story together, half each.
- **Voices.** **O** (older; serif text with a crescent moon, left) is slow and wistful, speaks in slightly old-fashioned images ("in the days when the moss was still young"), forgets things, and has a dry humour. **Y** (younger; hand-written text with a round star, right) is quick, bossy and silly, and makes the rules ("Wrong half. In my half, Blobb jumps higher than trees"). Ideally a player could take them for two odd story-creatures until the hospital hints start.
- **Y has authority over the story.** When Y objects, the world changes: Blobb's colour, the style of the scene, monsters, branches, physics. O goes along with it, often with a wry remark.
- **No drawing words in dialogue.** The narrators never mention drawing, crayons, paper or pencils, since that would make them human too early. They ask for a "simple", "bright" or "dark" world; the crayon look is the game's own interpretation of that.
- **Don't reveal WHO is in the hospital before ch3.** In ch1 and ch2, hints (beeps, gaps, "will you be here tomorrow?") must fit either narrator being the one who is unwell, or both, and any crack in one voice is balanced by one in the other. Ch3 ends that: O goes silent during the chase and Y calls for help (the director's decision).
- **Blobb** is "he" / "han". He is green; this was settled in ch1, where O said purple and Y corrected it.
- Lines are written in **English**, each with a Swedish (`sv:`) line under it. Chapters 1–3 and the UI strings are fully translated; when you add or change a line, write or update its `sv:` too (missing ones fall back to English). In Swedish, Y is everyday and bossy ("Nix", "Usch", "Vad var det jag sa"), O a little old-fashioned ("Nåväl", "Naturligtvis").
- **Tone arc.** Light-hearted and funny at first. Later chapters move toward childlike questions about what happens when we die. Hospital hints grow chapter by chapter; ch1 has only one quiet monitor beep at the very end.
- **Failure is a retell.** Y says "that's not what happened!" and the scene rewinds.

## Chapters

1. **The Other Side of the Forest** (done). Side-view forest platformer, then a top-down box-pushing puzzle in a clearing ("like a bird sees it").
   Beats: "Tell me something" → O's "somethings wander off, like cats" → O notices "the fuzzy one, over there" (a drawing or toy, left vague) → "I know half of his story; you tell the other half" (Y becomes co-narrator) → Blobb appears on blank paper → purple/green ("That was your half already") → a dark, realistic forest becomes a "simple" forest (shown in crayon) → "Blobb could jump super high" (jump unlocked) → pits with retells → a giant grumpy banana that Y shrinks → "the tree grew branches" → evening turns everything orange → "like a bird" fly-up into the top-down view → Blobb as a "fuzzy pancake" → crates and gate → a stream where "wooden crates float" (they become bridges) → Blobb rests → Y: "Will you be here tomorrow?" O: "...Where else would I be?" → "Tomorrow, Blobb learns to swim." → beep.
2. **Learning to Swim** (done). Pays off the ch1 promise ("Can Blobb swim?" "Not yet."). Set **outdoors**: the stream from the clearing runs down to an old mill's pond. Not an indoor pool, which would read as institutional and as a parent taking a child swimming. The look is realistic, not crayon.
   Pond (side view): murky water that Y makes "clear and sunny". Blobb sinks (his fuzz soaks up water) until "fuzz holds bubbles". He goes blue in the cold water (tint). The tiny grumpy banana is back as the pond warden ("No running!" "He's *swimming*."). Then river fish, a current, and a whirlpool (retells; no air meter, nothing like drowning).
   **Puzzle: the mill channel** (not box-pushing, no pipes). Three rooms of real streams: wheels open sluice spouts, levers turn boards, and the streams carry a grumpy rubber duck that floats up wherever no stream holds it. Blobb is stuck behind a fence, so he moves the water and the water moves the duck. Shortest solutions are 5, 8 and 13 presses (aimed at a 10-year-old). Room 1's duck lands on the banana's head and gets thrown straight back in. In room 3, Y decides water can run **backwards** (a wheel then cycles off, on, backwards); the room can't be solved before that.
   Ending: a drip throughout; one crack in each voice (O "...Where was he?", Y "...Lost my words"); "Where do bubbles go when they pop?" "Up. Into the air. Everywhere, a little."; then "You bring the lights. I'll bring the night." and two beeps.
3. **Lights** (done). Pays off ch2's "You bring the lights. I'll bring the night." The art is stylised (soft cel-shaded, painterly), not a drawing and not photoreal.
   Town (side view): a poster title page, then a street at dusk. O brings the night, Y switches the lamps on one by one. Under the lamps O makes Blobb gold; Y: "Wait. Wasn't he green?" O: "...Was he?" (**O's crack**: the colour settled in ch1 slips.) Then "Green, but shiny. Both." The banana is a street sweeper ("No loitering"). Blobb dashes (the drawn dash form) over a canal (retells), past sleeping pigeons, up a drainpipe.
   **Puzzle: light is solid** (rooftops, side view). Y: "In my half, light is solid." Cranks swing rooftop lamps; beams are bridges, 45° ramps or ladders. Light is one-way: Blobb jumps up through a beam but never falls through one. Turning a lamp you stand on drops you. Cranks are turned with E / Ⓑ (not jump). Three rooms, shortest solutions 2, 6 and 8 turns (checked against a generous jump, so there are no shortcuts). Room 2: stand on one lamp, turn the other. Room 3 has walls to climb ("Light goes through walls." "It doesn't, you know." "...Fine. Climb over them, then.").
   **The chase** (isometric). O's last line breaks off ("And up on the roofs, the birds were..."); Y finishes it, and **from here O says nothing**. Blobb runs across the roofs on the beat (lanes, jumps, chimneys, diving pigeons). Y talks alone, leaving gaps for O's half: "Your turn. ...Your half!", "You're doing the quiet thing again." The drum slowly becomes a monitor beep, then speeds up ("What is going on?"). The roofs end at a tall building with a plain lit sign (the hospital). Y: "...Help." "HELP!" The beeps stop; slow fade to white; chapter card.
   **This is where the frame shows, and it points at O.** Up to ch2 the hints were balanced between the two voices. From ch3 the player can read O as the one who is unwell. Ch4 should build on that (O's silence, Y alone or Y and a returning, quieter O), not walk it back.
4. **The Other Half** (done). Short. A soft dreamscape that never settles on one style; Blobb only walks, on a path that quietly loops, past drifting bits of earlier chapters (moss, the rubber duck, a ch3 street lamp). No puzzles, no danger, no retells.
   **Only Y speaks; O never answers.** Y opens with O's own line ("Where had we got to? ...That's your line."), tries to tell O's half in O's words ("Once, in the days when the moss was still young..."), and tries a rule: "In my half, it's morning. ...If it's morning, you must wake up." For the first time **nothing happens**; the story only works with both halves. Y drifts from talking about Blobb to talking to O ("I left the lights on. For you.").
   Ending: "...Dad?" / "Wake up." (sv: "...pappa?" / "Pappa, vakna.") This is the only family word in the game, and it recasts the narrators as a parent and child. Then the mist thins onto the mountain and the player makes a **short, simple climb** (a few ledges, no falling, no retells): **O climbing back to consciousness**. The monitor beep returns as Blobb climbs, faint and uneven at the foot, steadier with each ledge, slow and normal at the top. Nobody speaks on the climb; the steady beep is the answer. Chapter card.
5. **The Climb** (planned, not built). **The last chapter, and it ends hopeful: O recovers** (the director's decision). Side view, up the painterly mountain from the end of ch4, from night through blue hour to sunrise. Lighter again than ch3–4: jokes come back, and retells return, playful from both voices.
   **O comes back, faint.** Short lines, missing words; Y fills the gaps. Over the climb they become equals again, building the story half each, which is exactly what failed in ch4.
   **Mechanics (new, the director's choice):**
   - **Heartbeat ledges.** The monitor beep is now part of the climb: on each beat some ledges pulse and kick up like a springboard. Standing on one, or jumping on the beat, launches Blobb higher than a normal jump. At the foot the beat is uneven and hard to read; it steadies as O recovers, so the climb gets kinder as O gets better.
   - **Stretch and swing.** Glowing holds (roots, pegs) on the rock. Near one, press a button and Blobb stretches to it (the small flying oval from the drawing, `blobb_superjump`) and swings; let go to fling. Auto-aimed, so it's easy for a 10-year-old.
   **Structure.**
   1. *The foot* (night). O only in fragments ("...Where... had...", Y: "...had we got to. You're back."). Heartbeat ledges introduced, uneven.
   2. *The middle.* O's lines get longer; the dry humour returns. O makes a rule of their own for the first time, which unlocks stretch and swing.
   3. *The high stretch* (last stars). Equals again, half each. The pigeons from ch3 (cameo, the director's choice), friendly now, fly alongside. Near the top Y asks the softened question ("Where do stories go when they end?"); O answers in O's own images (ch2's "Up. Into the air. Everywhere, a little." to echo).
   4. *Summit.* Y: "In my half, it's morning." O: "In mine too." The sun comes up; the beep is slow and even.
   **Ending (the director's choice): a real-world glimpse.** After the sunrise, a brief, soft view of the hospital room in the morning: two silhouettes, and the drawing of Blobb on the wall. No words needed, or one last quiet exchange. Then the end card.
   **The question, softened** (the director's decision): not "What do you think happens when we die?" but something gentler in the same spirit, e.g. "Where do stories go when they end?"

## Blobb's forms (from the original drawing)

`assets/drawing.jpeg` shows Blobb seven ways. `tools/cut_drawing.py` cuts each one out of the photo into `assets/sprites/drawn_*.png`. Use the child's own figures wherever a form fits, rather than inventing new looks. In code: `BlobbSprite.set_form(name)`, in dialogue: `@blobb_form name` (`normal` goes back).

| Figure in the drawing | Asset | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Classic: green fuzz, purple dot eyes, unibrow, blue feet | `drawn_blobb`, animated strips | Blobb's normal self (ch1 on) |
| Small, stretched oval flying diagonally, legs trailing | `blobb_superjump` strip (AI frames 24–32) | The story-unlocked big jump (ch1 "higher than trees") |
| Green with a dark spiky crown, angry brows, jagged teeth | `drawn_grumpy` | Blobb gets prickly when the banana pushes him (ch1 `banana_bump`) |
| Round green scribble, no face, no legs | `drawn_ball` | Blobb seen from above, "a fuzzy pancake" (ch1 clearing) |
| Big grey pencil scribble-burst | `drawn_scribble` | A retell: Y scribbles out what happened (ch1 falls) |
| "BLOBB" in blue crayon letters | `drawn_title` | Title screen |
| Blue Blobb, open purple mouth, dark legs | `drawn_blue` | Not used yet. Ch2 avoids the crayon look and tints the animated Blobb with `BlobbSprite.BLUE` instead. |
| Green blur with speed lines, face out in front | `drawn_dash` | ch3 town: Blobb's dash over the canal (the chase itself uses a faux-3D Blobb drawn in code) |

Ideas for the rest: the scribble can also be weather (a storm cloud in ch3), and the ball form can roll down the ch4 mountain path.

## Style per scene

Mix styles freely, and let the narrators switch them: realistic, child's drawing, monochrome and so on. Backgrounds can change mid-scene when a narrator says so.
